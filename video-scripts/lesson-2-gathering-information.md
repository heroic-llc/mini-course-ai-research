Let's talk about gathering information with A I.

A I excels at helping you explore topics and find relevant information quickly. Here's how to use it.

Start broad, then narrow. Begin with general questions to understand the landscape. What are the main aspects of this topic? Then dive deeper into specific areas.

Ask for explanations. When you encounter something unfamiliar, ask A I to explain it simply. Pretend I know nothing about this. What's the basic idea?

Request multiple perspectives. Ask A I for different viewpoints on a topic. What are the main arguments for and against this? This gives you a more complete picture.

Get organized information. Ask A I to structure what you're learning. Can you give me this as bullet points? Can you create a simple framework?

Use A I to find what to search for. A I might not have the latest information, but it can tell you what to look for. What databases or sources should I check for current data on this?

Ask follow up questions. Research is a conversation. When A I tells you something, dig deeper. Why is that? What's an example? What are the exceptions?

The goal is to use A I to understand a topic efficiently before you dive into primary sources.
