Now let's look at synthesizing sources with A I.

Gathering information is just the start. The real value comes from making sense of it all. A I can help you connect the dots.

Summarize efficiently. Share an article or document with A I and ask for the key points. What are the main takeaways? This saves time on dense material.

Compare sources. When you have multiple perspectives, ask A I to identify similarities and differences. Where do these sources agree? Where do they disagree?

Find patterns. After gathering information from several places, ask A I what themes emerge. What patterns do you see across this information?

Create summaries for different audiences. Need to explain your research to others? Ask A I to summarize for a specific audience. Explain this for someone with no background in the topic.

Build on what you learn. Use A I to take your synthesis further. Based on this, what questions should I explore next? What implications does this have?

Organize your findings. Ask A I to help structure your research. Create an outline of what I've learned. Group these findings by theme.

Remember that synthesis requires judgment. A I can help you process information, but deciding what matters most is still your job.
